# EUR Receivable FX Hedge — Executive Recommendation

**To:** Chief Financial Officer  
**From:** Micah Yi, Treasury Analyst  
**Subject:** Recommendation for Hedging €4,500,000 EUR Receivable  

---

## A. Exposure Summary

The company expects to receive **€4,500,000** in approximately one year.

Because the company reports in U.S. dollars, the USD value of this receivable will change as the EUR/USD exchange rate moves.

The main risk is a decline in the euro before settlement. If the EUR weakens against the USD, the company will receive fewer dollars when the €4.5 million receivable is converted. If the euro strengthens, the USD value of the receivable increases.

Using the Stage 4 market data, the current EUR/USD spot rate is:

`1.1535 USD/EUR`

The analysis compares the following strategies:

- No hedge
- Forward hedge
- Money-market hedge
- EUR put option hedge

The call option was not treated as a primary hedge because the company's main exposure is to EUR depreciation.

---

## B. Hedge Outcomes

### Unhedged Position

The unhedged position gives the company full exposure to future exchange-rate movements.

At the current spot rate of:

`1.1535 USD/EUR`

the €4.5 million receivable is worth approximately:

**$5,190,750.00**

If the euro depreciates by 5%, the USD value falls to approximately:

**$4,931,212.50**

If the euro appreciates by 5%, the USD value increases to approximately:

**$5,450,287.50**

The unhedged position provides the greatest upside if the euro strengthens, but it also leaves the company fully exposed if the euro weakens.

---

### Forward Hedge

The forward hedge locks in approximately:

**$5,248,326.06**

at settlement regardless of the future EUR/USD exchange rate.

At a 5% EUR depreciation, the forward produces approximately:

**$317,113.56 more**

than remaining unhedged.

At the current spot-rate scenario, the forward produces approximately:

**$57,576.06 more**

than the unhedged position.

The main advantage of the forward is certainty.

The main disadvantage is that the company gives up the ability to benefit from additional EUR appreciation above the contracted forward rate.

---

### Money-Market Hedge

The money-market hedge produces approximately:

**$5,248,326.06**

at settlement.

This is essentially the same outcome as the forward hedge.

The results are nearly identical because the Stage 4 forward rate was calculated using covered interest parity from the same USD and EUR interest rates used in the money-market hedge.

The difference is mainly operational.

The money-market hedge requires the company to:

1. Borrow EUR today.
2. Convert the borrowed EUR into USD.
3. Invest the USD until settlement.
4. Use the future EUR receivable to repay the EUR borrowing.

Because the modeled proceeds are essentially identical to the forward, the money-market hedge adds more operational steps without creating a meaningful improvement in settlement proceeds.

---

### Put Option Hedge

The EUR put option provides protection against EUR depreciation while still allowing the company to benefit if the euro strengthens.

The put creates a minimum settlement outcome of approximately:

**$5,073,676.09**

after accounting for the settlement-date economic cost of the option premium.

The settlement-date premium cost is approximately:

**$117,073.91**

At a 5% EUR depreciation, the put produces:

**$5,073,676.09**

compared with:

**$4,931,212.50**

if the company remains unhedged.

The put therefore protects approximately:

**$142,463.59**

of settlement proceeds in that downside scenario.

The main advantage of the put is flexibility.

If the euro appreciates by 5%, the put produces approximately:

**$5,333,213.59**

compared with approximately:

**$5,248,326.06**

under the forward or money-market hedge.

The trade-off is the cost of the premium.

---

## C. Sensitivity Interpretation

The strategies behave differently depending on whether the euro depreciates or appreciates before settlement.

### EUR Depreciation

When the euro weakens, the forward and money-market hedges provide the strongest protection.

At a 5% decline in EUR/USD:

- No hedge: **$4,931,212.50**
- Forward: **$5,248,326.06**
- Money market: **$5,248,326.06**
- Put option: **$5,073,676.09**

The forward and money-market hedge protect approximately:

**$317,113.56**

relative to remaining unhedged.

The put also protects the company from a weaker euro, but its protected proceeds are lower because of the option premium.

---

### EUR Near Current Spot

At a terminal rate of:

`1.1535 USD/EUR`

the outcomes are approximately:

- No hedge: **$5,190,750.00**
- Forward: **$5,248,326.06**
- Money market: **$5,248,326.06**
- Put option: **$5,073,676.09**

At this exchange rate, the forward and money-market hedges provide the strongest modeled outcome.

---

### EUR Appreciation

When the euro appreciates, the trade-off changes.

At a 5% EUR appreciation:

- No hedge: **$5,450,287.50**
- Put option: **$5,333,213.59**
- Forward: **$5,248,326.06**
- Money market: **$5,248,326.06**

The unhedged position benefits the most from a stronger euro.

The put option also benefits from the appreciation because the company can allow the option to expire and convert the receivable at the more favorable market rate.

However, the put still trails the unhedged position because of the option premium.

The forward and money-market hedges remain fixed and therefore do not benefit from favorable EUR appreciation.

---

## D. Recommendation

I recommend using a **forward hedge for the full €4,500,000 EUR receivable**.

Based on the Stage 4 market inputs, the forward locks in approximately:

**$5,248,326.06**

at settlement.

This completely removes the company's downside exposure to EUR depreciation.

If the euro depreciates by 5%, remaining unhedged would produce approximately:

**$4,931,212.50**

while the forward would preserve:

**$5,248,326.06**

This protects approximately:

**$317,113.56**

of USD proceeds.

The money-market hedge produces almost exactly the same financial result, but it requires more borrowing, conversion, and investment activity.

Because the financial outcomes are essentially equal, the forward provides a simpler way to obtain the same economic protection.

The put option is also a reasonable strategy if management strongly values the ability to benefit from EUR appreciation.

However, the put carries a settlement-date economic premium cost of approximately:

**$117,073.91**

This reduces the protected outcome to approximately:

**$5,073,676.09**

Without a strong management view that significant EUR appreciation is likely, the additional cost of the option does not appear necessary.

For these reasons, the forward provides the strongest balance of:

- Downside protection
- Cash-flow certainty
- Budget stability
- Operational simplicity
- No upfront option premium

---

## E. Executive Justification

### Cash-Flow Stability

The forward hedge gives the company a known USD settlement amount of approximately:

**$5.248 million**

This reduces the risk that an unfavorable exchange-rate movement creates an unexpected cash-flow shortfall.

For a €4.5 million exposure, even relatively small changes in EUR/USD can have a meaningful effect on the company's USD proceeds.

A:

`$0.01`

movement in EUR/USD changes the value of the receivable by approximately:

**$45,000**

The forward removes this uncertainty.

---

### Budget Certainty

The forward also improves budgeting and financial planning.

By locking in approximately:

**$5,248,326.06**

Treasury and management can use a fixed USD amount when forecasting future cash flows rather than relying on an unknown future EUR/USD exchange rate.

This provides more predictable information for:

- Cash-flow forecasting
- Budget planning
- Liquidity planning
- Financial performance expectations

---

### Liquidity

The money-market hedge offers nearly the same financial result as the forward, but it requires more balance-sheet and cash-management activity.

The company would need to borrow EUR, exchange the funds into USD, and invest the USD proceeds.

The forward achieves a similar economic result without requiring these additional financing transactions.

This makes the forward easier to implement from a liquidity and operational perspective.

---

### Optionality

The main disadvantage of the forward is that the company gives up the ability to benefit from a stronger euro.

If EUR/USD increases significantly before settlement, the company must still convert the receivable at the contracted forward rate.

The put option avoids this limitation because the company can allow the option to expire if the market exchange rate is more favorable.

However, this flexibility is not free.

---

### Premium Cost

The put option has an initial premium cost of:

**$112,500.00**

When that cost is future-valued to the settlement date, the economic cost increases to approximately:

**$117,073.91**

This cost reduces the minimum protected proceeds under the put to approximately:

**$5,073,676.09**

The company would therefore need to place significant value on the possibility of EUR appreciation for the option's flexibility to justify its cost.

---

### Final Executive View

The unhedged strategy provides maximum upside but leaves the company fully exposed to a weaker euro.

The put protects against downside risk and preserves upside potential, but it requires a meaningful premium.

The money-market hedge provides essentially the same modeled result as the forward but requires more operational steps.

The forward provides the strongest combination of:

- Certainty
- Downside protection
- Simplicity
- Budget stability
- Predictable USD proceeds

For these reasons, I recommend hedging the full:

**€4,500,000 EUR receivable**

using a **forward contract**.

The Stage 4 forward rate used in this analysis was calculated through covered interest parity rather than taken from a directly executable dealer quote.

Before entering into the transaction, Treasury should obtain an executable market quote and confirm that the available forward pricing remains consistent with the economics shown in this analysis.

Subject to that final market confirmation, the forward hedge is the most appropriate strategy because it provides a known settlement value of approximately:

**$5,248,326.06**
